The notary

A notary is a public officer appointed by the State to receive the deeds that the parties must, or wish to, make authentic. The notary guarantees their date, keeps the original — the minute — and issues copies.

  • A deed that is proofIts content and date can only be challenged through a special procedure.
  • An enforceable deedWith the enforcement formula, it can be enforced without going to court.
  • A deed kept safeThe original stays at the office: you can obtain a copy years later.

How your file proceeds

  1. 1

    First contact

    By phone, WhatsApp or at the office: you describe the transaction you have in mind.

  2. 2

    Document list and estimate

    The office tells you which documents to provide and gives an estimate of the costs: registration duties, land registry fees, notary fees.

  3. 3

    Checks

    The office reviews the documents and runs the checks: land title, civil status, signatories' powers.

  4. 4

    Signing

    The deed is read and signed at the office, or by power of attorney if you cannot travel.

  5. 5

    Formalities

    Registration, then entry at the land registry or the trade register, as the case may be. You receive a certified copy of the deed.

Frequently asked questions

All questions
How can I avoid buying disputed land?

Prefer titled land and pay nothing until the notary has checked the title. The office requests a certificate of ownership from the land registry: it shows the current owner and any charges, such as a mortgage or an objection.

How much does a notarial deed cost?

Costs include duties and taxes paid to the State (registration, land registry) and the notary's fees, which are set by regulation. They depend on the type and value of the transaction: ask for an estimate before you start.

Can I buy land without travelling?

Yes. You give a power of attorney to a trusted person, who signs the deed of sale at the office in your name. The office then has the land title issued in your name.
