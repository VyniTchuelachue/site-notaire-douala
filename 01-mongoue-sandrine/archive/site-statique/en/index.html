What the office can do for you

In Cameroon, the notary is a public officer: notarial deeds are conclusive evidence and enforceable like a court judgment.

Land and property

Purchase, sale, exchange, long leases, mortgage loans. Before anything is signed, the office checks the land title and requests a certificate of ownership from the land registry.

Estates

Inventory of assets, division between heirs, transfer of land titles and company shares into the heirs’ names.

Family

Marriage contracts, including for binational and Franco-Cameroonian couples. Gifts to children or between spouses. Wills.

Companies

Incorporation under OHADA law (SARL, SAS, SA), capital increases, transfers of shares, minutes of general meetings.

Powers of attorney

So that someone you trust can sign on your behalf in Cameroon: buying land, settling an estate, a banking transaction.

Copies of older deeds

The office holds the records of Maître MBOBDA MONGOUE Elie. You can request a certified copy of a deed he executed.

If you live outside Cameroon

Buying land back home, settling a parent’s estate, setting up a company in Douala: most files can move forward without you having to travel.

  1. You write to us

    By WhatsApp or e-mail: what you want to do, the property concerned and the country where you live.

  2. We reply

    With the list of documents required and an estimate of the costs: registration duties, land registry fees and the notary’s fees.

  3. You send the documents

    First as scanned copies, so the office can review the file. The originals follow by post or through your representative.

  4. You sign, or someone signs for you

    During a visit to Douala, or by power of attorney: the office sends you the draft, which you sign before a notary in your country of residence or have legalised at the Cameroonian consulate.

  5. You receive your deed

    A certified copy of the deed, then follow-up of the formalities: registration, entry at the land registry or at the trade and companies register, as the case may be.

A Cameroonian notary is a civil-law notary, which is a different role from a notary public in the UK or the United States. A notarial deed is an authentic instrument: its date and content can only be challenged through a special procedure, and it can be enforced without going to court. The notary keeps the original, called the minute, and gives you certified copies.

Common questions

Can’t find your question? Write to the office.

Can I buy land in Cameroon without travelling?

Yes. You give a power of attorney to someone you trust, who signs the deed of sale at the office in your name. The office then has the land title transferred to your name.

How do I avoid buying disputed land?

Prefer titled land, and pay nothing until the notary has checked the title. The office requests a certificate of ownership from the land registry: it shows the current owner and any charges, such as a mortgage or an objection.

How much does a notarial deed cost?

Costs include duties and taxes paid to the State (registration, land registry) and the notary’s fees, which are set by regulation. They depend on the type of transaction and its value: ask for an estimate before you start.
